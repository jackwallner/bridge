Bridge Bidding for Beginners: Opening Bids and Responses

By Jack Wallner · October 12, 2026

Count your high-card points (ace 4, king 3, queen 2, jack 1) and open the bidding with about 13 or more. Open 1NT with a balanced 15 to 17, otherwise open a five-card major, otherwise your longer minor. As responder you need 6 points to bid: raise partner's major with three-card support, bid a new suit with 10 or more, and add your points to partner's to decide on game at about 25.

Count your points first

Bridge bidding starts with high-card points (HCP): ace 4, king 3, queen 2, jack 1. The whole deck holds 40, so an average hand has 10. Two numbers matter for the auction. Opening strength is about 13 points, and a partnership needs roughly 25 combined points for game (a major-suit game or 3NT) and 33 for a small slam.

Suits rank from low to high: clubs, diamonds, hearts, spades, then notrump. The two majors are hearts and spades, the two minors are clubs and diamonds. A bid names a level from 1 to 7 plus a suit or notrump. The level is the number of tricks above six, so 4♥ means ten tricks with hearts as trump.

Opening bids: pick the first call in this order

Pass with fewer than about 12 points. With 13 or more (many players also open a good 12), check the hand in this order. The first rule that fits is your bid.

  1. 1NT: 15 to 17 points and a balanced hand (4-3-3-3, 4-4-3-2 or 5-3-3-2). Many players still prefer to open a five-card major with 5-3-3-2.
  2. 1♠ or 1♥: five or more cards in a major. With five cards in both, open the higher-ranking spades.
  3. 1♦ or 1♣: otherwise, open your longer minor. With equal length, open diamonds, except with three clubs and three diamonds, where you open 1♣ so the auction stays low.

Hands of 20 or more points use stronger openings (2NT for a balanced hand of about 20 to 22, an artificial 2♣ for the very strongest hands). Those come after the basics.

Responding to a one-of-a-suit opening

You need 6 or more points to respond. With fewer, pass. Once you respond, you tell partner how strong you are by the level you choose.

3 or more cards in partner's major6 to 9Raise to 2 of the major (some partnerships stretch this to 10)
4 or more cards in partner's major10 to 12Jump to 3 of the major (invites game)

When partner opens a minor (1♣ or 1♦), look for a major first. With four or more cards in hearts or spades and 6 points, respond 1♥ or 1♠, bidding the cheaper major with two four-card majors. Without a major, raise partner's minor with real support or bid 1NT.

Partner opens 1♥. You have 6 HCP and three hearts. Raise to 2♥, the simple raise.

♠ K 5   ♥ Q 9 6 3   ♦ A 8 7 4   ♣ J 8 4

Partner opens 1♥. You have 10 HCP and four hearts. Jump to 3♥ to invite game.

♠ 8 4   ♥ J 7 2   ♦ K 9 6 5   ♣ Q 9 8 3

Partner opens 1♠. You have 6 HCP, two spades and no suit worth bidding at the 1 level. Respond 1NT.

Responding to 1NT

Partner has shown 15 to 17 points, so add your own. About 25 combined points makes 3NT. With 0 to 7 points pass, with 8 or 9 invite by bidding 2NT, and with 10 or more bid 3NT. Conventions such as Stayman and transfers sit on top of this, and partnerships agree on them in advance.

A caution about agreements

Everything above is a beginner Standard American framework. Real partnerships agree on ranges and conventions, and clubs differ on details like the exact limit of a simple raise. When you play with someone new, ask what they play.

Questions

How many points do you need to open the bidding in bridge?

About 13 high-card points opens at the 1 level, and many players also open a good 12. With fewer than that, pass and let partner speak.

What is a 1NT opening in bridge?

1NT shows 15 to 17 high-card points and a balanced hand, with no singleton or void and at most one doubleton. It tells partner almost everything about your hand in one call.

How many points do you need to respond to an opening bid?

Six. With fewer than 6 points you pass. With 6 to 9 you make a minimum response, with 10 to 12 you invite game, and with 13 or more you are strong enough for game.

What do you open with two five-card suits?

Open the higher-ranking suit. With five spades and five hearts open 1♠, then bid the second suit on your next turn. Opening the higher suit lets partner pick between them without raising the level.

How do you count high-card points in bridge?

Ace 4, king 3, queen 2, jack 1, and nothing for lower cards. The deck holds 40 in total, so an average hand has 10.

What is the best bridge bidding system for beginners?

Standard American is a good first system. It is what this page, Karen Walker's beginner guide (in the sources below) and Bridge Trainer use: five-card majors, a 15 to 17 point 1NT, and the longer minor otherwise. Whatever you learn, agree on it with your partner, because clubs and partnerships differ on details.

What do you bid in bridge with 20 points?

As opener it depends on shape. With a balanced 20 to 22 points, open 2NT. With an unbalanced 20, open one of a suit (Standard American one-level openings cover about 13 to 21 points) and show the extra strength on your next bid, because the artificial 2♣ is kept for hands of about 22 or more. If partner has already opened, 20 points puts the partnership in slam range, since about 33 combined points is the guide for a small slam.
