## Best Bridge App for Beginners

By Jack Wallner · Last updated October 18, 2026

**Best for a free course from zero:** Tricky Bridge: Learn & Play, a story-led series of 57 lessons. **Best for learning from a book-style course:** LearnBridge, with over 2,000 practice questions and a choice of Standard American or Two-Over-One. **Best for playing hands against the computer with every bid explained:** Bridge Mentor. **Best for online duplicate with rankings:** Fun Bridge. **Best for short drills on counting points, opening bids and card play:** Bridge Trainer, the app I make.

These apps do different jobs. Two teach, two let you play, and one drills, so the right pick depends on whether you need to learn the rules, play deals, or build habits between sessions.

**How we compared (October 2026):** I ran Tricky Bridge, LearnBridge and Bridge Mentor on a Mac, and read the App Store listing and a sample of recent reviews for Fun Bridge, which I did not run. For all five I checked ratings, update dates, devices and purchase types from Apple's lookup data. Prices are left out on purpose, because they vary by region and change.

**Disclosure:** Jack Wallner makes Bridge Trainer, one of the apps below. I tried to start every description with what the app does well.

## What is each bridge app best at?

### Fun Bridge

Fun Bridge is the most-rated bridge app here and is built for duplicate: you play South against the same AI partner and opponents as everyone else, then compare scores in rankings, leagues and daily tournaments. The listing also offers interactive lessons, commented deals from champions, replay and analysis, and configurable conventions. The trade-offs for a beginner: the best features sit behind a subscription, it needs an account, and a large share of recent one-star reviews cover account and server problems or the robot partner.

### Tricky Bridge: Learn & Play

Tricky Bridge teaches through 57 short lessons guided by three robot characters, and the listing says bridge is free to learn with no pop-up ads. It asks whether you have played before and starts with small hands rather than 13 cards. Beyond the lessons it has solo practice with a Hint button, robot duplicate tournaments, and missions and cosmetics. The trade-offs: I spent about a dozen taps in a comic-strip intro before the first lesson, you cannot jump to a topic, and practice and tournaments spend a deal currency that you buy.

### LearnBridge

LearnBridge reads like a textbook with questions. Packs are divided into chapters with page numbers and a midterm, a question must be answered before you turn the page, and a wrong answer shows the full count behind the right one. The listing mentions over 2,000 practice questions and both Standard American and Two-Over-One bidding. Three packs are free. The trade-offs: further packs are bought one at a time, there is no practice outside the chapters, and I saw no streaks or spaced review.

### Bridge Mentor

Bridge Mentor deals a full rubber-bridge hand the moment you open it. Its Hint button shows a grid of every bid you could make, what each one promises, and the recommended call highlighted. Tapping a seat shows an estimate of that hand from the bidding, you can undo back into the auction, and the listing says convention bids are highlighted. The trade-offs: the ranges are written in jargon, card-play hints highlight a card without a reason, there are no lessons or progress tracking, and free play stops after five deals a day.

### Bridge Trainer (my app)

Bridge Trainer has four free rooms: Card, Auction, Declarer and Defense. Drills cover counting high-card points, choosing an opening bid from a 13-card hand, planning the play and choosing opening leads, and the reason for each answer appears after you answer. There are no opponents and no account. Its limits are real. Bidding drills stop at the opening call (pass, a suit, or 1NT), so there are no responses or competitive auctions. Declarer and defense questions are single decisions, not a full deal. It uses beginner Standard American only, and it is new with no ratings yet. Endless practice, Fix My Mistakes and the timed challenge need Bridge+.

## Which should you pick?

- **You have never played and want a free guided start:** Tricky Bridge.
- **You prefer reading and answering questions at your own pace:** LearnBridge, especially if your club plays Two-Over-One.
- **You know the basics and want to play hands and see why a bid is right:** Bridge Mentor.
- **You want to compete on the same deals as other players:** Fun Bridge, once you are comfortable with bidding.
- **You are taking a class and want short reps between lessons:** Bridge Trainer. The [bridge bidding guide](https://jackwallner.com/ios/bridge/bridge-bidding-for-beginners.html) covers the same opening-bid rules with worked hands.
- **You want to avoid a robot partner:** Bridge Trainer and LearnBridge have none.

## Questions

### What is the best free bridge app for beginners?

Tricky Bridge teaches its lessons free, LearnBridge has three free packs, Bridge Mentor gives five free deals a day, and Bridge Trainer has four free practice rooms. Free tiers change, so check each listing.

### Which bridge app teaches bidding best?

Bridge Mentor explains every bid option on its Hint grid while you play, LearnBridge teaches Standard American or Two-Over-One in chapters with questions, and Bridge Trainer drills the opening bid with a stated reason for each answer.

### Is there a bridge app without a robot partner?

Yes. Bridge Trainer and LearnBridge are lessons and drills with no robot partner. Tricky Bridge, Bridge Mentor and Fun Bridge all include robot players.

### Can I play bridge on my iPad?

Yes. All five list iPad support in the App Store.

### Do I need an account?

Not for three of the five. Bridge Trainer, LearnBridge and Tricky Bridge showed no sign-up in my test. Bridge Mentor's duplicate modes use Game Center, and reviewers report that Fun Bridge needs an account.

### What is the easiest way for a beginner to learn bridge?

Start with short lessons on small hands before full deals, then practice between sessions. Tricky Bridge does this free, starting with small hands rather than all 13 cards, and the ACBL's Learn Bridge page calls it a free way for complete beginners to learn online at their own pace. If you learn better with a teacher, the same page helps you find a class or teacher near you.

### Where can I learn bridge online for free as a beginner?

The ACBL's Learn Bridge page lists a free one-hour Trickstarter course and points complete beginners to the free Tricky Bridge app. Among the apps here, Tricky Bridge teaches its 57 lessons free, LearnBridge has three free packs, Bridge Mentor gives five free deals a day, and Bridge Trainer has four free practice rooms. Free tiers change, so check each listing.
