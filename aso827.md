# Bridge Trainer ASO Audit

Audit date: 2026-08-27  
App Store ID: `6791026407`  
Primary market: United States  
Primary category: Education  
Secondary category: Games  
Implementation status: recommendation only, no metadata or screenshots changed

## Executive directive

Keep the `Bridge Trainer` product brand and the current App Store title. The title owns the app's clearest US query, #1 for `bridge trainer`, and supplies the bridge, training, learning, and bidding concepts without pretending the product is a full four-player game.

Replace the subtitle with `5-Minute Drills for Beginners`. The current `Bridge Bidding & Card Practice` repeats Bridge and the bidding concept already expressed by `Bid`, then spends the rest of the visible field on generic practice language. The proposed subtitle creates a strong beginner promise and makes the product's short-session difference visible.

Replace the hidden field with nonduplicated, literal bridge vocabulary. The field should form full compounds with `Bridge` from the title and `Drills` or `Beginners` from the subtitle. Preserve exact `bidding` coverage because `Bid` and `bidding` should not be assumed to stem identically.

Replace the screenshot package. The live iPhone story has only four frames, ends on a paywall with obsolete prices, and does not meet the shared six-to-eight-frame contract. Use six product-evidence frames with problem, mechanism, and proof first. Recapture a separate six-frame iPad set because Bridge genuinely supports iPad.

## Do not implement

- Do not rename the product to `Bridge Card Game`. The app is a trainer with drills, not a complete multiplayer or AI table.
- Do not claim online play, multiplayer, robots, tournaments, ACBL points, rubber bridge, Chicago bridge, live opponents, or customizable bidding systems.
- Do not use competitor brands such as Funbridge, BBO, Tricky Bridge, LearnBridge, or Bridge Baron in metadata.
- Do not promise mastery, guaranteed improvement, perfect bidding, or winning at the table.
- Do not imply every convention is covered. The beginner framework is Standard American and partnership agreements vary.
- Do not show subscription prices in screenshots or descriptions. Regional prices and current product configuration can change.
- Do not advertise a free trial without checking introductory-offer eligibility in the current purchase flow.
- Do not treat absent download reporting as zero downloads.

## Product truth and claim boundaries

Supported by current source and metadata:

- Four free rooms: Card, Auction, Declarer, and Defense
- Beginner Standard American framework
- Short mixed sessions and room-specific drills
- Realistic 13-card hands
- Explanations after answers
- Endless generated opening and point-count practice with Bridge+
- Spaced review of missed material
- Timed challenge and accuracy breakdowns
- Bridge Minute and Game Night Prep with Bridge+
- Native iPhone and iPad support
- Optional monthly, yearly, and lifetime Bridge+ products

Qualify or avoid:

- `Five-minute` describes the intended session design, not a guarantee that every flow finishes in five minutes.
- `No pressure` is supported by solo drills, but a timed challenge exists. Do not make an absolute no-timer claim across the entire app.
- `Never run out` is appropriate only on the Endless Practice frame with a local Bridge+ entitlement and the generated-hand implementation visible.
- `Fix My Mistakes` should not imply guaranteed learning retention. Use review and practice language.
- Do not imply all advanced conventions or partnership systems are supported.
- The current Quick Session does not include every Play scenario. Screenshot the explicit Declarer flow for card-play proof.

### Pitch substance

1. Benefit: know what to study next. Problem: bridge has too many concepts for a beginner to sequence. Advantage: Home gives one quick start plus four clear rooms. Proof: populated Home.
2. Benefit: make a confident opening decision. Problem: rules feel abstract away from a hand. Advantage: realistic hands turn a concept into one choice. Proof: readable 13-card hand and bids.
3. Benefit: understand the reasoning. Problem: a correct answer alone does not teach transfer. Advantage: immediate explanation stays attached to the same hand. Proof: correct state and coaching.
4. Benefit: make card-play decisions clearer. Problem: bidding-only trainers leave declarer play abstract. Advantage: card scenarios ask one concrete decision. Proof: free PlayDrillView.
5. Benefit: revisit weak spots. Problem: mistakes disappear after a session. Advantage: due review returns missed material. Proof: seeded review queue or Stats.
6. Benefit: practice beyond a finite question set. Problem: repeated static hands become memorized. Advantage: generated hands keep opening practice fresh. Proof: Endless Practice.

| Layer | Metric | Decision rule |
|---|---|---|
| Indexing | Ranks for title, subtitle, and hidden-field compounds | Protect #1 `bridge trainer`; confirm new `bridge drills` and beginner combinations before further visible changes. |
| Exposure | Search and browse impressions by storefront | Prefer qualified trainer and beginner queries over broad game traffic. |

| Activation | First session completed, second room opened, review item created | Distinguish curiosity from useful practice. |
| Retention | Next-day drill, due-review return, weekly active practice | Reject keywords that install but do not build a practice habit. |
